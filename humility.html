01 · Korean Culture

What does “lowering yourself” mean?

In Korean, “lowering yourself” does not mean thinking badly of yourself.

It often means not placing yourself too far above the other person. When you receive praise, you may soften your response, share the credit, or mention someone who helped you.

This can make the conversation feel less self-centered and can show humility, consideration, and awareness of the relationship.

Imagine this

Someone says:

“한국어 진짜 잘하시네요!”

“Your Korean is really good!”

You can simply say 감사합니다. But you may also hear 아니에요, 아직 멀었어요 — not because the compliment is unwanted, but because the speaker is responding modestly.

Important: There is no rule that Koreans must reject compliments. “감사합니다” is completely natural. The key is understanding why modest responses also appear so often.

02 · Lower yourself a little

아니에요, 아직 멀었어요.

“No, I still have a long way to go.”

This kind of answer can soften the compliment. The speaker is not necessarily denying reality. They are avoiding a response that may sound too proud or overly confident.

Real-Life Conversation

Situation: A friend compliments your Korean.

A

한국어 진짜 잘하시네요!

Your Korean is really good!

B

아니에요, 아직 멀었어요.

Not really. I still have a long way to go.

💡
Culture Point

Here, 아니에요 can mean more than a literal “no.” It can carry the feeling of “Thank you, but I don't want to praise myself too much.”

03 · Share the credit

다 같이 한 거예요.

“We all did it together.”

Humility can also appear when someone shifts attention away from themselves and includes the people who contributed.

Real-Life Conversation

Situation: A coworker praises your project.

A

이번 일 정말 잘하셨어요.

You did a great job on this project.

B

아니에요. 다 같이 한 거예요.

It wasn't just me. We all did it together.

💡
Culture Point

The speaker accepts the positive result but avoids presenting it as an individual achievement only. This can help preserve a comfortable sense of “we.”

04 · Acknowledge other people

선생님 덕분이에요.

“It's thanks to my teacher.”

Another common way to respond modestly is to mention the person who helped, taught, or supported you.

Real-Life Conversation

Situation: Someone notices that your pronunciation has improved.

A

발음이 정말 좋아졌어요!

Your pronunciation has gotten so much better!

B

선생님 덕분이에요.

It's thanks to my teacher.

💡
Culture Point

Instead of keeping all the credit, the speaker recognizes another person's contribution. That is one way humility can connect to relationships in Korean conversation.
