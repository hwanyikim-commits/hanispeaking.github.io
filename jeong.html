01 · Korean Culture

What is 정 (Jeong)?

정 is difficult to translate with one English word. It is not simply “kindness” or “love.”

It is closer to the feeling of care, attachment, familiarity, and connection that grows between people over time — often through small repeated acts of looking after one another.

That is why an ordinary Korean sentence can carry more meaning when you consider who is speaking, what relationship they have, and why the sentence is being said.

Imagine this

Your close Korean friend calls you and asks:

“밥 먹었어?”

“Did you eat?”

Sometimes they really want to know whether you ate. But depending on the relationship and situation, the question can also carry a feeling like “Are you doing okay?” or “I'm thinking about you.”

Remember: Not every friendly sentence is automatically 정. The relationship, shared history, and repeated care are what make the feeling meaningful.

02 · Care can sound ordinary

밥 먹었어?

“Did you eat?” — sometimes also a small act of care

With someone close, asking about food can be a practical question and a way of checking in at the same time.

Real-Life Conversation

Situation: Your friend knows you have been busy all day.

A

오늘 하루 종일 바빴지? 밥 먹었어?

You were busy all day, right? Did you eat?

B

아직. 이제 먹으려고.

Not yet. I'm about to eat now.

💡
Jeong Point

The words are about food, but the purpose can also be to check whether the other person has taken care of themselves.

03 · Care can become a small request

집에 가면 연락해.

“Text me when you get home.”

This sentence is not only about receiving a message. In a close relationship, it can communicate concern for the other person's safety and a desire to stay connected until they arrive home.

Real-Life Conversation

Situation: It is late and your friend is heading home alone.

A

나 이제 갈게.

I'm heading home now.

B

응. 조심히 가고, 집에 가면 연락해.

Okay. Get home safely, and text me when you get there.

💡
Jeong Point

The literal request is “contact me,” but the feeling behind it can be “I want to know you arrived safely.”

04 · Care can be something you give

이거 가져가.

“Take this with you.”

Food, fruit, side dishes, or small everyday items can become a way of expressing care without a long emotional speech.

Real-Life Conversation

Situation: You are leaving a friend's or family member's home.

A

이거 가져가. 집에서 먹어.

Take this with you. Eat it at home.

B

아, 괜찮은데. 고마워!

Oh, you don't have to. Thank you!

💡
Jeong Point

정 often becomes visible through small repeated actions — giving food, checking in, remembering preferences, or looking after someone in practical ways.

05 · Shared experience

“I know what you mean.”

Common ground can help people feel closer

Finding out that you went to the same school, worked in the same field, or went through something similar can quickly create familiarity and empathy.

🤝
Culture Point

A shared experience is not automatically 정. But it can create the feeling of “I've been there too,” which can help a relationship grow and make 정 easier to develop over time.
